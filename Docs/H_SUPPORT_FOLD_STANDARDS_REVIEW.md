## Visible assessment

The19–45 sequence establishes a distinct load and origin-side preparation. The hilt moves through delivery; hip/chest turn supports the action, and87–117 retains a low carry and separate return. This meets the basic whole-action construction requirement. The eased apex is represented in source spacing; its perceived duration and release acceleration remain unscored without continuous review.

The weakest sampled interval is63–81, particularly69–81. The left shoulder/elbow treatment improves the shape modestly, but the arms still form a broad band across the upper chest close to the face. The support arm looks arranged to accommodate the grip rather than effortlessly carrying the weapon. This is an artistic interpretation of the silhouette, not proof of an anatomical or IK defect. Visible wrist angles and hand overlap also prevent a high deformation score. Grip closure is numerically good, but that does not prove convincing wrist articulation.

The corrected neutral reference shows opening into a shoulder-side load and outward lateral delivery. It does not support keeping hands close throughout. Our raised load and crossing mass need a clearer shape relationship to the torso; exact world-space height or joint angles cannot be recovered from the reference. Its obscured return cannot certify our carry fidelity.
