### Armor family findings and Hound emphasis

The family shares natural underlying proportions, curved metal shells with deliberate ridges, thin construction edges, dark flexible joints, restrained leather/brass hardware and muted cloth. The silhouettes vary meaningfully: Kestrel sweeps rearward, Gateward is compact and enclosed, Wayfarer spreads horizontally at the brim, Watchman uses a strong face opening, and Hound projects forward. Lancet narrows through the waist while Bell emphasizes rounded chest volume; Field Captain and Border Rider use larger textile areas. Sweeping Spaulders, Split Fauld, Ridgeline Greaves, Riverbend, Lockwrist, Mitten and Lamellar studies extend the same material vocabulary through different plate coverage and articulation patterns. Matching the style permits these differences; it does not require identical armor outlines or all-metal costumes.

| Hound feature | Visible observation | Proposed production/review implication |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Pointed crown and pronounced projecting visor, with a narrow black eye slit | Preserve crown/visor profile and slit readability at opponent distance before adding small detail. Confirm the same identity from front and oblique views |
| Major planes | Convex skull contrasts with angular visor faces and a distinct central ridge | Build continuous intentional forms; preserve both curvature and plane changes. Neither a featureless smooth dome nor a uniformly triangulated shell expresses the reference |
| Steel | Large irregular angular value regions sit within broad warm highlights and cooler reflections | Preserve visibly authored stylization. Test whether patch boundaries remain subordinate to volume in motion; do not reduce the target to generic smooth PBR or treat every patch as a physical edge |
| Material hierarchy | Bright steel against dark mail/padding; a large quiet blue chest region; small leather and brass accents | Maintain readable separation and a resting area for the eye. Mail texture must not overwhelm the head or shimmer at gameplay distance |
| Construction cues | Circular visor pivots, sparse breathing holes, thin rims, visible straps and layered mail | Model only required silhouette/depth detail; infer unseen construction explicitly. Check neck motion and visor/shoulder clearance during the selected exchange |
| Multi-view consistency | The main and side views communicate the same concept, but hole placement/count and some local construction details do not align exactly | Resolve one coherent 3D design before detailed modeling. Treat images as concept evidence, not mechanically exact orthographic plans |

**Interpretation, not a statement of Daniel's reasons:** Hound's appeal plausibly comes from the combination of a distinctive profile, strongly designed metal planes and rich steel/mail/cloth contrast. Its surface variation is more assertive than the quieter longitudinal reflections of Riverbend and the larger calm regions of Mitten. All remain user-approved style references. The Notion page's older smoother-Steel_v025 caveat and the accepted isolated Unreal material remain useful implementation context; they must not erase the newly emphasized Hound image. Preserve existing accepted assets while translating this range into the future guide. A static illustration cannot establish how those patches should respond to changing light or temporal sampling.

### Confirmed target-art-direction statement

**A sunlit medieval melee game with naturally proportioned, individually designed armor and weapons; distinctive sculpted silhouettes and coherent steel reflections enriched by deliberate angular surface variation, with Hound Bascinet as the preferred armor-study exemplar; warm stone, cool metal, dark mail and muted heraldic cloth; clear routes and strong landmarks; crisp first-person hands, blades and opponent poses at gameplay distance; economical geometric effects and a clean, world-compatible interface. Motion and sound should make weight, intent, contact and consequence immediately understandable while preserving responsive player control.**

Essential priorities: original knight/armor family; readable complete combat frame; material separation; warm/cool daylight; intentional detail hierarchy; expressive connected motion; restrained feedback. CS sharpness must come from useful forms and stable presentation, not artificial halos. Riot concept softness belongs primarily to distant atmosphere. Zelda and Valorant UI principles should be translated into the knight's restrained medieval material/shape vocabulary; neither interface should be copied wholesale. The user's exceptional-sound assessment is a target preference; audio quality and synchronization have not been independently evaluated in this intake.

## Material language and sharpness — separate decisions

**Primary success criterion:** the user wants the Valorant/Arcane authored texture aesthetic without the appearance of actual paint. After viewing v020, the user clarified that the texture was too crowded and requested a smoother surface. Broad calm steel with a few restrained angular reflections is the current target; dense faceting is not success. The original painted-map v002 candidate scored 4/10 and remains historical evidence. Current candidates use `python Tools/BuildStylizedSteelStudy.py --revision 026`, with continuous steel geometry, per-part object-space normal textures, broad influence masks and protected construction edges. Editable Blender scenes, maps, settings, generator snapshots and independent critiques are saved by revision. See [iteration record](../ArtSource/StyleReference/MEL17/ITERATION_LOG.md). This is a bounded material test within MEL-17, not full-character production or user acceptance.

**User clarification during this work:** Icebox is disliked; Split is acceptable, while **Mirage and Anubis are preferred for sharp, clear geometry and outstanding colors**. Mirage/Anubis lead the environment brief. Split supplies a secondary authored-material/color reference. The earlier Icebox and Dust II selection is withdrawn from the review sheet.

The sheet uses Valve's historical Mirage B-wall before/after example from [The Mirage Process](https://blog.counter-strike.net/2013/06/7149/), an Anubis screenshot from [its creators' Workshop page](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1984883124), and Riot's Split concept comparison from [The Creation of Split](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-creation-of-split/). These are explicitly labeled historical screenshots/concept art, not current CS2 build evidence. The precise preferred map version remains unspecified; do not let these historical examples override the user's preference for outstanding color and sharp geometry.

Our proposed translation: authored steel patch shapes, modest roughness contrast and selective bright rims; quiet warm medieval masonry, crisp silhouettes, stable texture detail and clear hand/blade edges at gameplay resolution. Still images can establish spatial detail, but motion review must establish temporal sharpness. Avoid artificial sharpening halos, excess bloom, motion blur or shallow depth of field. Generic noisy PBR, soft painterly blur and a cel-band shader would each miss different parts of this combination.
