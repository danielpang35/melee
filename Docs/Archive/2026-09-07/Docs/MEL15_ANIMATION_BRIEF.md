============================================================
FIRST PRINCIPLE: ANIMATE A HUMAN, NOT A SWORD WITH ARMS ATTACHED
============================================================

The sword must appear to be generated by the body.

The body should not look like it is solving two hand constraints around a mathematically predetermined sword.

A convincing two-handed cut should communicate a kinetic chain resembling:

grounded stance / weight pressure
    →
pelvis
    →
thorax
    →
clavicle + scapular motion
    →
glenohumeral shoulder motion
    →
upper arms
    →
elbows
    →
forearms
    →
hands / grip
    →
hilt
    →
blade

The exact magnitude varies by strike, but that causal relationship must be visible.

The viewer should intuitively perceive:

"I can see how this person generated that sword velocity."

Right now that is not true.

Fix it.

============================================================
LATEST VISUAL FAILURE TO INVESTIGATE
============================================================

The newest 30-fps side-by-side comparison is approximately 2.2 seconds long and shows the current attempt against the prior state.

The most important visible defects are:

1. ELBOW DIRECTION / HUMERAL PLANE

The elbows flare, rotate, or travel through directions that do not look anatomically motivated by the hands, shoulders, or cut.

At several phases the arms read like elbow pole vectors being forcibly satisfied rather than articulated human limbs.

Investigate whether:

- elbow pole computation is reversed
- side/sign conventions are wrong
- coordinate spaces are mixed
- anatomical handedness corrections exposed an old sign assumption
- wrist-derived pole vectors are producing bad humeral rotation
- shoulder-to-hand geometry becomes underconstrained
- near-extension causes elbow-plane instability
- the elbow solve has discontinuities or flips
- identical/symmetric rules are being incorrectly applied to asymmetric lead/trailing arms
- grip constraints are dominating silhouette quality
- shoulder accommodation is compensating in the wrong plane

Do NOT simply add an arbitrary elbow offset until the frame looks better.

Determine the real reason.

2. THE ARMS LOOK CRAMPED

The sword travels dramatically while the hands and upper arms often remain crowded near the torso.

That creates the original "short arms" / forearm-driven appearance from MEL-15.

The solution is probably not longer bones.

Audit real rig proportions first.

Then solve the actual posing.

3. THE SHOULDERS DO NOT CONVINCINGLY OWN THE ARMS

A human sword swing involves substantial scapulothoracic/clavicular organization.

Do not allow the upper arm to behave as if it emerges from a static ball joint glued to the chest.

Check:

- clavicle motion
- scapular implication through the shoulder anchor
- shoulder protraction/retraction
- shoulder elevation/depression
- thoracic rotation
- shoulder girdle translation
- how armor alters the perceived shoulder center

The shoulder should remain attached to the torso while still accommodating reach.

4. THE HILT PATH IS NOT ENOUGH

A mathematically smooth hilt arc can still produce atrocious animation.

Do not judge the attack primarily by the blade trajectory.

Judge:

- silhouettes
- body mechanics
- hand path
- elbow path
- shoulder behavior
- rhythm
- arcs
- spacing
- overlap
- follow-through
- weight
- balance
- tension/release
- attack readability

5. THE SWING LACKS HUMAN ANIMATION RHYTHM

Avoid uniform interpolation.

The attack should have deliberate temporal structure:

ANTICIPATION / LOAD
→
COMMITMENT
→
EARLY RELEASE ACCELERATION
→
BLADE PASSAGE THROUGH THE TARGET
→
CARRY / FOLLOW-THROUGH
→
DECELERATION
→
RECOVERY

The release should feel decisive.

Do not spend most of the attack slowly rotating toward the target and then snap the last few degrees.

The early/middle portion of the released swing should carry substantial acceleration and hand travel.

============================================================
USE THE SEVEN-PART SWING MODEL
============================================================

Evaluate every candidate swing through these seven coupled animation dimensions:

1. HILT TRANSLATION

Where do the hands actually move through space?

Do not fake a huge blade cut with almost-stationary hands.

2. BLADE ORIENTATION

Blade orientation should emerge coherently from body/hand motion.

Avoid excessive wrist-generated rotation.

3. ANTICIPATION

Create a readable loading pose that stores potential energy without becoming theatrical or sluggish.

4. ELBOW + HAND PATH

This is currently the critical failure.

Author/elicit believable continuous trajectories.

The elbows should not simply chase the wrists.

Each elbow should move as part of a plausible shoulder/upper-arm plane.

Lead and trailing arms must behave differently when biomechanics demand it.

Avoid:

- elbow wings
- elbow inversion
- symmetrical robotic flaring
- sudden pole-vector flips
- elbows crossing strangely across the sternum
- humeri rotating backward while the hands travel forward
- excessive tucked compression
- full-extension locking
- collapsing wrists

5. EARLY-RELEASE ACCELERATION

The sword should acquire meaningful velocity early enough that the swing feels committed and dangerous.

6. FOLLOW-THROUGH

Momentum continues after the target plane.

A miss must not magically stop because the gameplay damage window ended.

7. RECOVERY PATH

Recovery should grow naturally out of the finish.

Do not teleport/interpolate straight back to guard in a way that erases momentum.

============================================================
ANATOMICAL STANDARD
============================================================

Become rigorous about the anatomy.

For each arm, reason about:

- clavicle
- shoulder center
- humerus
- elbow hinge axis
- forearm pronation/supination
- radius/ulna orientation
- wrist
- grip
- sword handle contact

The elbow is NOT a free-floating pole marker.

Its position is a consequence of:

shoulder placement
+
humeral elevation
+
humeral rotation
+
elbow flexion
+
forearm orientation
+
hand target

During overhead preparation it is acceptable for elbows to elevate.

It is NOT acceptable for them to become arbitrary lateral wings.

During release, the elbows should transition continuously as the hands travel through the cut.

They should progressively organize the arms behind the hilt trajectory instead of corkscrewing around the torso.

Do not impose one universal elbow direction for the entire strike.

Do not impose one universal elbow direction for every strike family.

Attack-specific elbow rails / preferred planes are acceptable if that produces better animation.

Authored procedural animation is preferred over mathematically generic animation.

============================================================
OVERHEAD REQUIREMENT
============================================================

MEL-15 specifically requires the overhead to FINISH THE CUT WITH THE HANDS.

Do not call it follow-through because the blade tip points at the ground.

On an unobstructed neutral-pitch overhead miss:

- hands travel forward
- hands travel DOWN through the target
- shoulders participate
- elbows extend in a controlled fashion
- thorax participates
- the hilt visibly clears the high chest
- the grip should finish roughly below the sternum toward upper abdomen / waist, adjusted intelligently for this rig and weapon
- THEN recovery begins

The current high-hands / folded-elbows finish is unacceptable.
